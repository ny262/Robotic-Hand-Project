Project Overview

The goal of this project is to design an anatomically accurate hand, controlled by a muscle sensor and actuated by a servo-tendon system, which is able to open and close a fist.

The project is divided into four phases, each with its own set of objectives and deliverables. The first three phases are detailed below, while the fourth phase will be addressed in future work.

The design of the robotic hand is based on the anatomical structure of the human hand, specifically focusing on the muscle-tendon system that enables finger movement.

  • The human muscle-tendon system sits in the forearm and anchors at the wrist carpal ligament, providing the mechanical foundation for finger curling.
  • Muscle contractions pull these tendons downward, rotating the phalangeal joints to close the fingers into a fist.
  • The main finger flexor is a deep forearm muscle mass that splits into four separate tendon lines to control each finger independently.

Development Stage

Prototyping Methodology

Mechanical Insights & Limitations
Low-Fi Modelling Constructed hand profile using corrugated cardboard cutouts. Bonded rigid cardboard tube sections to simulate bone structures while leaving joint creases unglued for joint mobility. Cardboard compliance under structural loading highlighted a definitive need for rigid, 3D-printed phalanges to prevent body warping during execution gestures.
Tendon Actuation Routed dental floss strands through the cardboard alignment tubes to act as flexor tendons, manually pulling the strands to model flexor muscle contractions. High friction and binding tendencies between the dental floss and tube walls demonstrated a critical requirement for dedicated, low-friction tendon guide channels in the final CAD design.

Table 2: Mechanical Limitations Gauged from the Low-Fidelity Prototype and Solutions

While the cardboard model worked as a quick proof of concept, the testing process highlighted several mechanical limitations that helped guide the final CAD design choices.

Problem

Cause

Solution
Fingers don't curl at the right angles Using a single servo means all the tendon strings get pulled at the exact same distance. Switch to multiple servos to control each finger independently.
Thumb and pinky bend the wrong way The cardboard prototype can only bend flatly in a single direction. Design angled hinge joints in the final CAD model to mirror real hand movements.
Fingers stay permanently bent The cardboard creases after a few pulls and loses its natural spring. Use a closed-loop tendon system that physically pulls the fingers back open when the servo reverses.
Servo motor topples over under a heavy load The motor generates more torque than the temporary tape and mount can handle. Model custom mounting brackets directly into the 3D-printed forearm chassis.

The objective was to translate standard human hand dimensions into a functional mechanism. The digital design focused on overcoming three main structural constraints:

  • Shifting away from flat, single-axis hinges toward angled joint rotations to replicate realistic finger paths.
  • Moving from a single, shared motor line to a dedicated multi-servo system to allow individual finger control.
  • Routing internal pathways for a continuous, closed-loop tendon cable that acts like a pulley to actively pull the finger back open during servo reversal.

Phalangeal Linkage Design

  • An interlocking clevis design stops lateral joint wobble and increases overall structural stability.
  • Counterbored pivot holes allow the hinge pins to sit completely flush, keeping the outer profile smooth.
  • Internal routing channels run through the core to guide the high-tensile tendon lines smoothly.

Figure 4: Close up of the phalangeal linkage design, highlighting the interlocking clevis and internal tendon routing channels. Self-sourced.

Palm Casing and Tendon Alignment Channels

  • Designed as a two-piece split chassis to allow straightforward internal access for routing the tendon lines and mounting the hardware.
  • Integrated alignment channels guide each finger along its natural flexion path and prevent the tendon lines from tangling or slipping.
  • Snap-fit latches help the top and bottom halves attach easily, ensuring perfect assembly alignment without requiring excessive fasteners
